Every night, countless humans look up at the stars and wonder about our place in the cosmos. What we see in that vast darkness - all the stars, planets, and galaxies - is just the beginning of a much deeper story. A story where 95% of our universe is invisible, yet its presence shapes everything we can see.

Think about walking through a crowded room with your eyes closed. You can't see the furniture, but you know it's there because you bump into it. The universe works similarly - we can't see dark matter directly, but we can feel its gravitational effects on everything around it.

Let's start our journey with a simple question: How do we know something is there when we can't see it?

Imagine spinning a ball on a string. The faster you spin, the harder the ball pulls outward. Now picture our galaxy - a massive spiral of stars spinning through space. Based on what we can see, the outer stars should be moving slower than the inner ones, just like planets move slower the further they are from the Sun. But they don't. They move much faster than they should.

This is our first clue that something invisible is at work. Let's look at the math that reveals this mystery:

For a star orbiting the center of a galaxy, its velocity (v) should depend on its distance (r) from the center according to:

$$ v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} $$

Where:
- G is the gravitational constant (nature's rule for how strong gravity is)
- M is the mass inside the star's orbit
- r is the distance from the center

If we only count the visible matter, this equation tells us outer stars should move slower. But they don't - they move at nearly the same speed regardless of their distance. This means there must be more mass out there than we can see.
